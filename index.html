Seven staged routes. Each assumes you start where you are, spends money only where it changes the outcome, and reaches a first income before it reaches a certificate. Pick the one whose starting line matches yours.

The rule that sits above all seven

Under every scenario for how AI develops — fast or slow — three things hold their value: foundational literacy and numeracy, human and socio-emotional skill, and physically embodied or accountability-bound work. Those are the no-regret investments. Everything else is a bet on a specific future.

The pairing that wins in both the WEF and LinkedIn data is one technical skill + one human skill. A welder who can quote a job and manage a client out-earns a welder who can only weld. An analyst who can present out-earns one who can only query.

1 · No degree, need income in 90 days

1

Weeks 1–2 · Get documented

Aadhaar, PAN, bank account, e-Shram registration, DigiLocker. Register free on the National Career Service. If you already have work experience, apply for Recognition of Prior Learning — it certifies skill you already have, free, with a ₹500 payout on passing.

2

Weeks 2–6 · One fast credential

Pick by what is near you: forklift operator (1 week), FSSAI FoSTaC food safety supervisor (2 days, ~₹1,000–2,500), retail or warehouse NSQF course, security guard training, or delivery-partner onboarding for immediate cash while you train.

3

Months 2–4 · Enter the trade ladder

Apply for a NAPS apprenticeship. Minimum stipends (revised September 2025): Class 10 pass ₹8,200, Class 12 or ITI ₹9,600, diploma ₹10,900, graduate ₹12,300 per month. This is paid learning with a completion certificate.

4

Months 4–18 · Compound it

Move toward a licensed trade — the state electrical wireman/supervisor licence is the single highest-leverage credential available without a degree, because the licence legally gates who can sign off work. Plumbing, HVAC and welding follow the same logic.

2 · Class 10 or 12 pass, want a real career

1

Choose the ladder, not the job

Government ITI (1–2 years, ₹500–7,000 a year) or a polytechnic diploma (3 years after Class 10, with lateral entry into year 2 of B.Tech). Both are cheap and both have onward routes. Free curriculum and question banks: Bharat Skills.

2

Pick a trade with an upgrade path

Electrician → licensed supervisor → contractor. Machinist → CNC programmer → tool room. Welder → 6G/ASME IX → Gulf. Fitter → industrial maintenance → automation. Avoid trades with no visible next rung.

3

Add the two force multipliers

Spreadsheets and spoken English. They are free, they take 8 weeks each, and they are what separate a technician from a supervisor. Around 90% of Indian youth cannot enter a spreadsheet formula — this is cheap differentiation.

4

Then decide: depth, licence, or abroad

Depth pays in tool rooms and automation. A licence pays in contracting. Abroad pays most — NDT Level II or 6G welding turn ₹25,000–80,000 of training into Gulf earnings of ₹1.5–3 lakh a month.

3 · Graduate, want AI-resilient white-collar work

1

Months 1–3 · Build the floor

SQL (4–8 weeks, free) is the highest job-yield-per-hour skill available to a non-engineering graduate. Add Power BI (PL-300, ~₹4,865) — highest interview-call rate of any data credential in India. Publish 3 dashboards on real public data.

2

Months 3–6 · Attach AI to a domain

Prompt and context engineering is not a job on its own in India. Attach it to something: finance, healthcare, legal, supply chain, marketing. Then learn verification — checking AI output is now the human half of the job, and it is what gets paid.

3

Months 6–12 · Pick an accountability moat

Go where a named human must sign: audit, AI governance, data protection (DPDP), safety, quality, regulated finance. IAPP AIGP for AI governance; NISM for finance; ASQ/IASSC for quality. Accountability cannot be delegated to a model.

4

Throughout · Ship proof, not certificates

foundit (July 2026): roughly 60% of Indian postings now expect prior internship experience, and internships/contract roles rose from 12% of postings in 2024 to 32% in 2026. A public portfolio beats a paid certificate almost every time.

4 · Engineering graduate facing a soft IT market

Read the market honestly. TCS ended FY26 with 23,460 fewer employees year on year. NASSCOM's Strategic Review 2026 shows FY26 revenue up 6.1% against headcount growth of just 2.3% — revenue and headcount have decoupled. Entry-level IT postings were down about 10% year on year in July 2026 (foundit). Fresher hiring has stabilised, not recovered.
1

Do not compete on writing routine code

That is the most substituted software task and the reason junior hiring fell. Compete on what carries production risk: DevOps, cloud, data engineering, security, embedded systems.

2

Target GCCs, not just services firms

2,117 GCCs across 3,728 units employ about 2.36 million people (Zinnov–NASSCOM, data to March 2026), and 96% of centres set up after FY2021 launched with product and transformation mandates. Chennai and Hyderabad showed the strongest 2026 GCC hiring growth. Most GCC hiring runs through LinkedIn and referrals, not job boards.

3

Use apprenticeship as the side door

NATS graduate apprenticeships pay a minimum ₹12,300 a month with government reimbursing half the stipend — employers say yes far more readily than to a direct hire.

4

Consider the physical side of engineering

Electronics manufacturing (22 approved projects, ~34,000 direct jobs, January 2026), semiconductors, EV, solar and industrial automation all need engineers who will stand on a shop floor. Far less competition than software.

5 · Want to work overseas

1

Pick the corridor, then the skill

Gulf — cheapest and fastest: welding, NDT, HVAC, crane, nursing, hospitality. Germany — nursing and eldercare via the free Triple Win programme; also Ausbildung. Japan/Korea — SSW and EPS-TOPIK; JLPT costs only ₹1,400–2,600. UK/Australia — nursing, slower and dearer.

2

Language is the gate, not the skill

Germany needs A2 before departure and B1/B2 for recognition (Goethe A1 ~₹8,500, B1 ~₹11,500, B2 ~₹15,000). Japan needs JLPT or JFT-Basic. IELTS is ~₹19,000. Budget 6–12 months for language before anything else.

3

Get your qualification recognised early

Nurses: state council registration, then NOC and verification — it takes months. Trades: third-party qualification tests (ASME IX, ISO 9712, NCCCO) matter far more than an Indian course certificate.

4

Verify every agent before paying anything

Check the recruiting agent's registration on eMigrate. Fees are legally capped at 45 days' wages or ₹20,000. Sub-agents are illegal under the Emigration Act, 1983. Pay by cheque or DD with a receipt — never cash or UPI. NSDC International is free and government-backed.

6 · Women re-entering or entering work

The gap is widening, not closing. The India Skills Report projects female employability falling to 47.5% in 2026 while male rises to 53.5%. The ILO finds women nearly twice as likely as men to be in the highest AI-exposure category (4.7% vs 2.4%), because of concentration in clerical roles. Female LFPR reached 32.7% (June 2026 monthly bulletin) but growth has come largely through agriculture and unpaid family work.
1

Free, women-first training

Jan Shikshan Sansthans are free, doorstep, and about 83% of beneficiaries are women. Government ITIs are fee-free for women at NSTIs. Skill India Digital Hub courses are free.

2

Skills that pay without commuting

Tailoring and boutique work, beauty and wellness (home service), ECCE and home daycare, bookkeeping and GST filing, medical coding, data annotation, and content or design freelancing.

3

The two standout schemes

Namo Drone Didi — free DGCA drone pilot training plus up to 80% or ₹8 lakh drone subsidy for women in self-help groups. Triple Win nursing — free language, placement and relocation to Germany at €2,200–3,500 a month.

4

Formalise the work you already do

Udyam registration is free. It converts home-based work into a business that can access Mudra credit, GeM procurement and PMEGP subsidy.

7 · Rural, small-town or farming background

1

Use the free rural training network

Krishi Vigyan Kendras run 3–15 day trainings for ₹500–10,000 on mushroom cultivation, beekeeping, soil testing, dairy, protected cultivation and food processing. Nearly free, in your district, and under-used.

2

The high-return rural skill right now

Agri-drone piloting: a DGCA Remote Pilot Certificate takes 5–7 days and ₹25,000–1,00,000 at an approved RPTO. Owner-operators earn ₹50,000–1,50,000 a month in season. Free for women via Namo Drone Didi.

3

Sell to institutions, not individuals

Smallholders cannot pay much. FPOs, agri-businesses, cold chains and processors can. The 10,000 FPO programme created thousands of organisations that cannot find professional managers — MANAGE trains for exactly this.

4

Use the capital subsidies

PMFME gives 35% subsidy up to ₹10 lakh for micro food processing. National Horticulture Board subsidises polyhouses at 50%+. PMMSY funds aquaculture. Skill plus subsidy beats skill alone.

Jobs — government

  • National Career Service — free umbrella portal: private and government vacancies, free career counselling, 200+ Model Career Centres, rojgar melas. Explicitly states no fee at any stage. Links through to eMigrate for overseas work.
  • State employment portals — only trust .gov.in or .nic.in domains (for example sewayojan.up.nic.in). Many "rojgarsangam" and "sewayojan" lookalike sites are unofficial clones running ads or harvesting data.
  • Central exams — SSC, UPSC, Railway Recruitment Boards, IBPS for banking, state PSCs.
  • PSU and research — DRDO, ISRO, NTPC, ONGC, BHEL, SAIL career pages; most engineering entry runs through GATE scores.
  • Health — state National Health Mission portals carry the largest volume of nursing, ANM, CHO and technician vacancies.
  • Aggregators like sarkariresult and freejobalert are unofficial — useful for alerts, but always confirm on the .gov.in source before paying any fee.

A certificate says you attended. Proof says you can do the work. Indian employers increasingly test rather than trust — India Skills Report 2026 puts national employability at 56.35%, and the gap between credential and competence is exactly what they are screening for. Here is what counts as proof in each cluster.

The four kinds of proof, ranked by what employers actually believe

1

Statutory licence or registration

Strongest of all, because it is legally required and independently verified: state electrical supervisor licence, DGCA Remote Pilot Certificate, state nursing council registration, SEBI RIA registration, AMFI ARN, IRDAI agent licence, BEE energy auditor, FSSAI FoSTaC, PSARA compliance. Nobody argues with a licence.

2

Third-party performance test

A test you can fail, marked by someone with no interest in you passing: ASME IX / ISO 9606 welder qualification, ASNT NDT Level II, CKA (hands-on Kubernetes), RHCSA (practical exam), NCVT trade test, Prometric and DHA nursing exams, CPCT typing.

3

Public work with a real audience

A GitHub history with merged pull requests to repositories you don't own. A published dashboard someone else uses. Two Play Store apps with reviews. Kaggle Competitions Expert tier. A campaign with real spend and real ROAS. A YouTube channel with retention data. This beats most paid certificates.

4

Vendor certification

Useful as a screening key, weak as evidence of ability: AWS SAA, PL-300, CCNA, Security+, Salesforce Admin, PMP, NISM. Necessary to get past the filter; never sufficient to pass the interview.

How to verify a course or institute before you pay

  • Look up the qualification pack on the National Qualifications Register. Search by sector, NSQF level or QP name. If the course claims NSQF alignment and the QP is not on NQR, it is not aligned. This single check prevents most training-fee fraud.
  • Check the awarding body on NCVET. As of December 2025, NCVET recognised 229 entities — 161 awarding bodies and 68 assessment agencies. If the institute's awarding body is not among them, the certificate has no national standing.
  • For ISO lead auditor courses, demand the CQI-IRCA registration number or Exemplar Global certification and verify it on their site. Unregistered lead auditor courses are worthless and common.
  • For Six Sigma, ask who sets and marks the exam. If the answer is "we do", walk away. Only ASQ, IASSC or a genuine employer belt programme (GE, Honeywell style) carries weight in India.
  • For SAP, verify the training partner on SAP's own site before paying ₹2–3 lakh. Unaccredited SAP institutes are a well-established fee-extraction target for fresh graduates.
  • For aviation academies charging ₹1.5–2 lakh with vague placement promises — apply directly to airlines first. Most train their own cabin crew free once you are selected.

Credentials to avoid or treat with suspicion

  • "Certified Prompt Engineer" and AI-agent certificates from non-vendors. Indian employers assign these zero value. Vendor credentials (Google's Generative AI Leader at $99) or a working public repo are the only recognised proof.
  • TensorFlow Developer Certificate — the programme is closed. Do not pursue it.
  • "Figma certified" — no official Figma certification exists. Portfolio only.
  • Self-issued Six Sigma belts — most Indian Six Sigma certificates come from training firms with no independent exam.
  • CEH — roughly ₹1.1 lakh and widely considered overpriced, but it remains an HR keyword in Indian IT services and PSU tenders. Buy it only if a specific job advertisement names it.
  • AMCAT (~₹949) and eLitmus (~₹950) — paid employability tests with a shrinking employer base. Declining relevance.
  • ISC2 Certified in Cybersecurity — the free "One Million Certified" offer ended 20 May 2026. Existing codes are valid until 31 December 2026; new candidates pay $199 plus a $50 annual fee.

Why FIRA matters: export of services is zero-rated under GST, but the exemption is only defensible if you can prove receipt in convertible foreign exchange. The FIRA or FIRC is that proof. Collect it for every payment, every time.

Tax and compliance for freelancers

  • GST registration threshold: ₹20 lakh of annual services turnover (₹10 lakh in special-category states). Below that, registration is optional even if all your clients are foreign.
  • File a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) annually if you export services — it lets you invoice without paying 18% IGST upfront and claiming it back.
  • Section 44ADA presumptive taxation — declare 50% of gross receipts as income, no books required. Limit ₹50 lakh, raised to ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are 5% or less of turnover (which is the case for anyone paid digitally). Available to individuals and partnerships, not LLPs or companies.
  • Advance tax is due quarterly once your liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a year. Missing it costs interest under sections 234B and 234C.
  • Keep separate accounts — a dedicated current account for business receipts makes the 44ADA and GST position defensible and makes you creditworthy.

Formalising a micro-business

  • Udyam registration — free, online, Aadhaar-based. This is the document that unlocks bank credit, GeM procurement and most subsidy schemes.
  • Mudra loans — Shishu, Kishor and Tarun tiers for micro-enterprise capital without collateral.
  • PMEGP — capital subsidy for new micro-enterprises, higher rates for women, SC/ST and rural applicants.
  • PMFME — 35% capital subsidy up to ₹10 lakh for micro food-processing units.
  • PM Vishwakarma — for traditional artisans and craftspeople: toolkit incentive, collateral-free credit and skill training.
  • GeM and ONDC — government and open-network procurement channels that give small sellers access to institutional buyers.

For the roughly 90% of Indian workers in the informal economy, this paperwork is frequently worth more than another technical skill. It converts the same work into documented, creditworthy, contractable income.

Job fraud in India escalated sharply through 2025–26, and the newest variants can make you criminally liable rather than merely poorer. Read this before you pay anyone anything.

The one rule that catches most of it

No legitimate Indian employer, and no government portal, ever charges a jobseeker a fee. Not for registration, not for training material, not for a security deposit, not for police verification, not for a laptop. The National Career Service states this explicitly. If money flows from you toward the job, stop.

The one legal exception is overseas recruitment, where a registered agent may charge a capped fee — see below.

The six live scam patterns

  • Registration and processing fees. Any payment demanded before onboarding. Often dressed as a "training kit", "uniform deposit" or "background check".
  • Telegram and WhatsApp task scams. Like, rate or review tasks with small early payouts to build trust, followed by demands to "recharge" for higher-value tasks. One Karnataka case involved ₹85 lakh lost.
  • Money-mule recruitment — the 2026 escalation. A "remote job" asks you to open a bank account and hand over the passbook, debit card and SIM "for verification". The kits are trafficked and used to launder proceeds. This makes you criminally liable, not just a victim.
  • Fake offer letters impersonating TCS, Infosys, Accenture or Amazon — spoofed letterheads, Gmail or lookalike domains, artificial deadlines.
  • Fake overseas jobs. Unregistered sub-agents, a tourist visa instead of an employment visa, contracts shown only on arrival. Linked to trafficking into Southeast Asian scam compounds. Fake "Triple Win" agents charging fees are a specific known scam — the real German nursing programme is free.
  • Clone government portals. Non-.gov.in "Rojgar Sangam" and "Sewayojan" sites harvesting Aadhaar and personal data.

Verifying an employer — the seven-minute check

  • Is the email from a corporate domain, not Gmail or Outlook? Check when the domain was registered.
  • Does the same vacancy appear on the company's own careers page? If not, treat it as fake.
  • Does the recruiter have a real LinkedIn profile with history and mutual connections?
  • Is the company registered on MCA, or does it have a verifiable GST number?
  • Will they do a video interview? Scammers avoid faces.
  • Never share Aadhaar, PAN, OTPs, bank credentials or your passport before a verified written offer.

Overseas recruitment — the legal position

  • Demand the original Registration Certificate issued by the Protector General of Emigrants and verify the RA number on emigrate.gov.in.
  • Fee cap: 45 days' contract wages, ceiling ₹20,000. Anything above this is illegal.
  • Pay by cheque or DD only, and always take a receipt. Never cash or UPI.
  • Sub-agents are illegal under the Emigration Act, 1983 — and most fraud originates with them.
  • Prefer free government-backed routes: NSDC International and, for nursing to Germany, Triple Win.

If it has already happened

  • Report within the golden hour. National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: cybercrime.gov.in · Helpline 1930. Faster reporting materially improves the chance of freezing the money.
  • Overseas recruitment fraud: MEA complaints against recruiting agents.
  • If you handed over bank credentials, inform your bank immediately and file a police complaint — that record is what separates a victim from an accused in a mule investigation.

Things to verify before you commit money or time

Suryamitra may be between cycles. As of August 2026 the NISE portal shows no ongoing batches (2,330 completed, 69,901 trained since 2015) and MNRE's page has not been updated since 2022. Contact NISE directly before planning around free solar training. SCGJ-affiliated private partners run the same qualification pack for a fee.
BEE energy auditor registration closed on 3 August 2026. The next National Certification Examination cycle is expected in 2027. The study material is free on the exam dashboard — use the interval.
PMKVY 4.0 is under-delivering. 18.79 lakh certified against 33.47 lakh enrolled — about 15.65% of its three-year target as of December 2025. A Cabinet re-sanction for FY 2026-27 could not be confirmed in writing. Do not build a plan around securing a free PMKVY seat.
PM Internship Scheme figures conflict across sources. The original 2024 design specified ₹5,000 a month for 12 months; PIB and 2026 coverage state ₹9,000 minimum for 6–9 months, with age bands variously reported as 18–25 and 21–24. Check the official portal.
ASDC does not publish course fees. Get a written quote before enrolling in any EV or automotive programme.

Where the numbers are soft

  • Salary figures. Government and company-published figures (Urban Company's partner earnings index, apprenticeship stipend gazettes, exam fees) are firm. Most role-level salary bands come from staffing and consulting sources with undisclosed methodology, and should be read as ranges, not quotes. No credible 2026 India benchmark exists for cybersecurity analyst, standalone data analyst, digital marketer, nurse, electrician, welder, solar technician or delivery partner pay — the figures circulating are SEO aggregations.
  • "Agent orchestration engineer" salaries. Every published India figure for this and similar new AI titles traces back to SEO or training-institute content with no methodology. The underlying AI-engineer bands are real; the specific new titles are not yet benchmarked.
  • Green jobs projections range from 3 million to 35 million because India has no standard classification of a "green job". Definitions and horizons differ across ILO, IRENA, SCGJ and CRISIL.
  • Semiconductor employment. India Semiconductor Mission's own FY2026-27 targets are modest — about 1,500 in fabs and 3,000 in ATMP/OSAT. The widely repeated "one million semiconductor jobs" claim is unsourced.
  • GCC numbers (2,117 GCCs, 2.36 million professionals) come from Zinnov–NASSCOM, an industry body, not official statistics.
  • Two job-market trackers disagree for the same month. Naukri JobSpeak recorded white-collar hiring up 5% in July 2026; foundit's tracker recorded postings down 12% year on year. Both are directionally useful; neither is authoritative. Use both.
  • India Skills Report "employability" (56.35%) is a score from the Wheebox/ETS Global Employability Test administered to test-takers — a constructed measure, not measured unemployment.

What changed since the source document

  • Labour data. PLFS Annual Report 2025 (released 27 March 2026): unemployment 3.1% on usual status, youth 15–29 at 9.9%, urban youth 13.6%. Agriculture's employment share fell to 43.0% from 44.8% — reversing the rise the source document flagged. Monthly bulletin (June 2026): unemployment 5.5% on current weekly status, LFPR 54.4%, female LFPR 32.7%. The two series use different bases and must not be mixed.
  • IT hiring. TCS closed FY26 down 23,460 employees year on year; Infosys and HCLTech shrank in the June 2026 quarter while TCS and Wipro added. NASSCOM's FY26 review shows revenue up 6.1% against headcount growth of 2.3% — the decoupling is now explicit. Fresher hiring has stabilised, not recovered.
  • AI hiring is genuinely growing. Naukri JobSpeak: AI/ML roles up 33% year on year in July 2026, the strongest category in Indian white-collar hiring. LinkedIn's Skills on the Rise 2026 (India) names prompt engineering, workflow automation and LLMOps among the fastest-rising skills.
  • Entry-level evidence, India-specific. foundit (July 2026): entry-level hiring down 10% year on year; internships and contract roles rose from 12% of postings in 2024 to 32% in 2026; about 60% of postings now require prior internship experience; AI-led roles grew from ~1% to 4% of fresher postings. Note the counter-evidence: Naukri recorded fresher hiring up 6–17% through 2026, largely outside IT.
  • There is no WEF Future of Jobs Report 2026. The 2025 edition remains current. Any article citing a "2026 report" is recycling 2025 figures.
  • Apprenticeship stipends were raised by gazette effective 11 September 2025, and NATS rates again from 1 April 2026.
  • Free credentials that closed: ISC2's free Certified in Cybersecurity offer ended 20 May 2026; the TensorFlow Developer Certificate programme closed; Chegg India's Q&A programme shut down on 18 March 2026.

How to use this

The Skills tab is a filter, not a ranking. There is no single best skill — there is the best skill for your starting point, your capital and your geography. Filter by what you already have (education, money, time), then read the note on each card, because the note is where the honest caveat lives.

Two habits matter more than any choice you make here. First, verify before you pay — the National Qualifications Register, NCVET's recognised-body list and eMigrate exist precisely because fee fraud is endemic in Indian skilling and recruitment. Second, build proof in public from week one, because the Indian market has shifted decisively from screening credentials to screening evidence.
